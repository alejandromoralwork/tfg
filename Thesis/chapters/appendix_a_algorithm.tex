\chapter{Batch Clearing Algorithm}
\label{app:algorithm}

The clearing routine implements \eqref{eq:demandsupply} and
\eqref{eq:clearing} by a search over the limit prices submitted to the
batch, followed by the tie-break cascade and the rationing step of
\Cref{sec:rationing}. \Cref{sec:implfba} describes the same routine in
words, together with the data structures behind it.

\begin{lstlisting}[language=,caption={Batch clearing.},label={lst:clearing}]
INPUT : bids B and asks A, each order with limit pi (none for a
        market order), remaining quantity q and submission time t;
        rationing rule R; previous clearing price p_prev
OUTPUT: clearing price p*, fills F, residual E

1  candidates <- distinct limit prices in B and A
2  if candidates is empty: return (no clearing, all orders roll over)
3  for each p in candidates:
4      D(p) <- market buy qty + sum of q over buy limits with pi >= p
5      S(p) <- market sell qty + sum of q over sell limits with pi <= p
6      V(p) <- min(D(p), S(p))
7  Vmax  <- max over p of V(p)
8  M     <- { p : V(p) = Vmax }                      // the set P*
9  M     <- argmin over p in M of |D(p) - S(p)|      // tie-break step 2
10 if |M| > 1: M <- argmin over p in M of |p - p_prev|  // step 3
11 if |M| > 1: M <- argmin over p in M of p             // step 4
12 p*    <- unique element of M
13 eligible buys  <- market buys and buys with pi >= p*
14 eligible sells <- market sells and sells with pi <= p*
15 sort each side by R (here: market first, better pi, earlier t)
16 match the two sorted lists front to front at p* until one
       side is used up; record each fill in F
17 E     <- every order with quantity left
18 roll E over into the next batch; if Vmax > 0, p_prev <- p*
19 return (p*, F, E)
\end{lstlisting}

Steps 1--8 are the price search: $D$ and $S$ only change value at a
submitted limit price, so the volume-maximising price is always among the
candidates, and since $D$ is non-increasing and $S$ non-decreasing the
maximising set is an interval. A batch with no limit order at all has no
price of its own and does not trade (step~2). When $V_{\max} = 0$ the
remaining steps produce no fill, so every order rolls over as well.
Steps 9--11 are the tie-break cascade: minimise the residual imbalance,
then the distance from the auction's own previous clearing price
$p_{\mathrm{prev}}$ (not an external reference, since the dataset has no
oracle price), then take the lowest price. Step~15 is the only place where
the rationing rule enters, so a different rule would change the allocation
without touching the price computation. Because step~16 walks each side in
one priority order rather than first filling every inframarginal order and
then rationing the marginal ones, an order priced strictly better than
$\clr$ is filled completely in the typical case but not always: when the
maximising interval spans more than one price, a better-priced order on the
\emph{longer} side can be left only partly filled (\Cref{sec:testfba}).
